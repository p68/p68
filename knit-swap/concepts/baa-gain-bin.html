Hello and welcome, dear knitter!

Come in, come in, and mind the wool on the floor. This is my little corner of the internet where knitters swap, sell and give away their leftover yarn, needles, patterns and finished samples. Every one of us has a bag in the cupboard that is too good to bin and too odd to use up. Here it goes to somebody who will love it.

I built this site in 2005 after a Computers for the Over-Sixties evening class (thank you, Gareth) and I have been fiddling with it ever since. It is not fancy. It does work, mostly. The new "Near You" map shows who is selling in which village, so you can pop round with a flask instead of paying postage.

Happy knitting, Margery P. (retired, Class 3, Ewe Green C of E Primary)

Pattern of the Month

Dolly's Hot-Water-Bottle Cover (the sheep one, by popular request). Easy, mostly garter stitch, and a good way to use up odds and ends.

Yarn: about 250 g Aran (180 m / 197 yds per 100 g), cream plus a scrap of dark for the face. Needles: 5 mm / UK 6 / US 8. Tension: 17 sts and 34 rows to 10 cm in garter.
Row 1: cast on 52 sts, knit every row for 18 cm, then bobble row: k4, (MB, k3) to end. Repeat bobble row every 6th row. Full pattern when I find the other half of the page.

    Swap Rules

    Not rules, exactly. More the things I wish somebody had told me in 2005 before the Great Moth Incident.

    1. Be honest about the wool. Say what it is: fibre, weight, grams left, yardage if you know it. "Some kind of green" is a start, but not a good one.
    2. Mention the dye lot. Two balls from different lots will stripe your jumper in a way you did not plan. If you have lost the label, say so, and I will not tell anyone.
    3. Smell it. A bag from the loft that smells of the loft goes in the freezer for 48 hours before it goes anywhere near my cupboard. Moths are not welcome at the bin.
    4. Swaps should feel fair. A 100 g skein for a 100 g skein, DK for DK, or something near enough. If you are not sure, a biscuit helps.
    5. Keep prices kind. We are not a shop. Charge what you paid, or less, and do not ask for postage you have not worked out.
    6. Meet somewhere sensible. Porches, the market, the café. If you are meeting a stranger for needles, tell someone where you are. Bring a friend if you can, and knit.
    7. Needles: say the size twice. In millimetres and in the old UK number, and the US one if you can: 4 mm / UK 8 / US 6. It saves tears.
    8. Wind it before you hand it over. A tangled hank is a gift of work. Wind it into a ball or a cake, or at least tie it loosely in four places.
    9. Say thank you. Sign the guestbook and tell me what you made. I want to see the photographs, even the ones where the sleeve is odd.
    10. If it goes wrong, be nice. Everyone here is somebody's nan, or somebody's grandson, or the vicar. We all started with dropped stitches.

    That's all! Now go and rummage. — Margery
