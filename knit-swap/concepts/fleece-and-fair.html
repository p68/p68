        Fleece to Yarn

        Every skein on the Swap Table started life on a sheep's back. Here's the journey, in order, with one honest tip from the kitchen table at each stop.

        1. Shear

          Mabel's tip

          Clip in early summer on a dry day. Keep the fleece in one piece, cut side down, and leave the dags behind the barn door.

        2. Wash

          Mabel's tip

          Hand-hot water and a squirt of washing-up liquid. Soak, don't swish. Felting comes from fuss, not from water.

        3. Card

          Mabel's tip

          Tease the locks open first, then card in thin batts. Wensleydale is happier combed than carded.

        4. Spin

          Mabel's tip

          Park and draft, then let the twist run up. Ply back the opposite way and the yarn will sit up straight.

        5. Dye

          Mabel's tip

          Onion skins or madder, simmered never boiled. Write the dye lot on a tag before you forget which pot was which.

        6. Knit

          Mabel's tip

          Wash and block a swatch before you trust it. For DK, try 4mm (US 6) and go up a size if your fabric is stiff as a gate.

        Don't fancy the first five? Skip straight to the Swap Table and join the story at step six.

        Good tagging

        Weigh it, count the yardage, and say whether it came from a smoke-free home. Honest dye-lot notes get swapped quickest. Write needle sizes in mm and US so everybody's happy.
